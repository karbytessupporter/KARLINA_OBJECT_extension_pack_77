## The immediate conflict

On October 7, 2023, Hamas and allied Palestinian armed groups attacked southern Israel, deliberately killing civilians as well as security personnel and abducting approximately 250 people. Israel responded with a massive military campaign whose stated aims were recovering the hostages and destroying Hamas’s military and governing capacity. That campaign killed tens of thousands of Palestinians, displaced nearly Gaza’s entire population, and destroyed much of the territory.

A ceasefire beginning in October 2025 greatly reduced full-scale warfare. All surviving hostages were released, and the final hostage’s remains were recovered in January 2026. But the ceasefire did not produce a political settlement. The present Gaza deadlock concerns:

* Whether and how Hamas and other armed organizations will disarm.

* Whether and when Israel will withdraw completely.

* Who will govern Gaza afterward.

* Whether an international security force will be deployed.

* How Gaza will be reconstructed and who will finance it.

* Whether Palestinians displaced within or from Gaza may return.

* What security guarantees Israel will receive.

Israel insists that Hamas must be disarmed before complete Israeli withdrawal; Hamas has accepted a proposed phased process in principle but contests Israel’s interpretation and the disposition of Palestinian weapons. Israeli attacks and Palestinian militant activity have continued despite the ceasefire, while Gaza remains devastated and overwhelmingly dependent on aid. [Reuters summarized the current deadlock in August 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trumps-gaza-plan-hinges-hamas-disarmament-israeli-withdrawals-2026-08-10/), and the [latest UN humanitarian report](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-4-september-2026) documents continuing strikes, civilian deaths, displacement, infrastructure collapse, and aid restrictions.

## The conflict underneath that war

A simplified historical sequence is:

1. **Late nineteenth century through British rule:** Zionism developed as a Jewish nationalist movement seeking a secure homeland in Jews’ ancestral land, especially amid severe European antisemitism. Palestine already contained a predominantly Arab population, alongside longstanding Jewish communities. Increased Jewish immigration, British imperial policies, and incompatible nationalist aspirations produced escalating conflict.

2. **1947–1949:** The UN proposed dividing Palestine into Jewish and Arab states. Most organized Jewish leadership accepted partition; Palestinian Arab and neighboring Arab leadership rejected it as an unjust division of an overwhelmingly Arab country. War followed. Israel was established and expanded beyond the proposed UN boundaries. Approximately 700,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled—the **Nakba**, or catastrophe. No Palestinian state emerged; Jordan controlled the West Bank and Egypt controlled Gaza.

3. **1967:** Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza. Palestinians have subsequently lived under varying forms of Israeli military control, annexation, settlement, blockade, or restricted autonomy.

4. **1990s Oslo process:** Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization recognized one another, and the Palestinian Authority received limited administration over portions of the West Bank and Gaza. Borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, and security were postponed for later negotiations that never produced a final agreement.

5. **Since 2007:** Palestinian politics has been divided. Hamas controlled Gaza, while the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority administered portions of the West Bank under overarching Israeli control. Neither represents every Palestinian. Likewise, Israel’s government does not represent every Israeli or every Jewish person.

The [UN’s historical overview](https://www.un.org/unispal/history/) provides a more extensive chronology.

## The unresolved core questions

* **Palestinian statehood:** Will there be a genuinely independent Palestinian state in Gaza and the West Bank, with East Jerusalem as its capital? Or will Israel retain permanent control?

* **Borders and settlements:** Most two-state proposals use the pre-1967 boundary—the Green Line—with negotiated land exchanges. Israel has established extensive civilian settlements beyond that line. Palestinians see them as seizure and fragmentation of their prospective country. The UN Security Council and International Court of Justice regard the settlements as violations of international law; Israel rejects the ICJ’s conclusions and invokes historical claims, security, and negotiation. [The ICJ’s 2024 findings and Israel’s response are summarized here](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/top-un-court-deliver-opinion-israels-occupation-palestinian-territories-2024-07-19/).

* **Jerusalem:** Israel considers Jerusalem its unified capital. Palestinians seek East Jerusalem as their capital. The city also contains sites sacred to Jews, Muslims, and Christians.

* **Palestinian refugees:** Palestinians displaced in 1948 and their descendants seek some combination of return, restitution, compensation, and acknowledgment. Israel rejects an unrestricted return into Israel because it could eliminate the country’s Jewish majority.

* **Security and freedom:** Israelis cite massacres, suicide bombings, rockets, kidnappings, and October 7 as evidence that relinquishing territory can create mortal danger. Palestinians cite occupation, blockade, military raids, imprisonment without trial, home demolitions, settler attacks, and land confiscation as evidence that “security” is being used to perpetuate domination.

* **The ultimate political system:** The traditional proposal is two states. Alternatives include one democratic state with equal citizenship, a confederation, or continued Israeli control. The last option increasingly resembles permanent rule over millions of Palestinians who cannot vote for the government exercising decisive authority over them.

There is a profound power asymmetry: Israel is a sovereign state with a powerful military and extensive territorial control; Palestinians are politically divided and lack sovereignty. Recognizing that asymmetry does not make attacks against Israeli civilians legitimate. Correspondingly, Hamas’s October 7 atrocities do not erase Palestinian rights or relieve Israel of its obligations toward civilians.

So the deepest question is not simply “Which side owns the land?” It is: **what political arrangement can provide Jewish Israelis with security and collective self-determination while providing Palestinians with freedom, equality, justice, and collective self-determination—and can it do so without expelling or permanently subordinating either population?** No accepted arrangement currently exists.
