I was in the midst of donwloading some YouTube videos to watch offline later tonight and throughout the upcoming weekend and also doing some (batch) WayBack Mackine saves of existing canon KARBYTES files outside in the backyard and laying on the laid stones area north of the wooden deck adjacent to The Burrow (instead of on the deck because it apparently has too high visibility (to the extent that it apparently caused some neighbors to close their blinds until I was no longer hanging out line of sight to their windows and, also, I think people in my house were passive-aggressively trying to get me to leave the deck last night by turning on the flood lights until I left)). I like being down here on the ground more anyway, so I'm not really upset.

What I noticed is that, at approximately 11:40AM Pacific Standard Time on 10_SEPTEMBER_2026, several mammals have gathered along the fence to rest in the shade within my line of sight. One of them was a black cat with yellow-green eyes and the other two were red tree squirrels. I was thinking about how fortunate I am to share this backyard space with those creatures. 

I was thinking that an older and dumber version of me would have boasted that it felt like the "transcendent cosmic intelligence" of nature reaching out to me to make me feel befriended. Then I thought about the many disturbing true stories I've been learning about online including the story of some girl who was approximately 18 years old and got locked in the basement of her house by her father who kept her prisoner there without anyone else knowing until she was rescued approximately twenty years later and after her father had raped her and she birthed incestuous offspring. I thought about how no cutesy squirrels, cats, and song birds would find her there for those decades she was shut away from the light of day and open air.

So I no longer like saying things like "cosmic intelligence" or anything else which seems "spiritual". To me, the word "spiritual" has unsettling connotations; namely, the connotation of there being some kind of fundamental and universal moral direction in nature. In reality, I think that morality is always limited to biased subjective points of view only because, in order for one organism, X, to live, it seems that some other organism, Y, has to be consumed, killed, or otherwise forced into relative squalor by X. And if the information processing agents (i.e. biological, mechanical, or even purely logical (i.e. simulated or hypothetical)) are not sentient (or at least do not experience pleasure or pain), then those agents would still seem to have to displace other potential beings in space, time, matter, and energy because only one agent can occupy a respective slot at a time. In other words, a universe seems to only have a finite capacity per finite space-time interval to house any agents. I think that, in order for something to arise in a material sense rather than remain noumenal, that being has to crowd out competing forms (and that's more fundamental of an "ethic" than are more complex human-centric notions of moral righteousness and evil).

Is nature fair? I would say not absolutely and not inherently; but some partitions of nature might seem fair to some agents.

Is nature obliged to promote any being's happiness? I would say not necessarily. I would say that happiness is simply the result of certain conditions overlapping such that happiness is its emergent property; but not a property that can be sustained outside of relatively fragile circumstances (which seems to require a constant energetic input rather than only passivity on the part of the respective sentient agent).

The point of this note is to remind myself and others that karbytes has gotten to a point in its philosophical development where it no longer uses the term "spirituality" and, instead, simply uses the term "philosophy" in its place. I define philosophy as being the mental activity of inquiring about what is true and not true and which experiences are subjectively worthwhile (to specific subjective points of view) and which are not. Philosophy gives me something to do rather than be purely passive and at the mercy of my environment. Philosophy gives my brain questions to focus on attempting to answer. Obtaining the correct answer is not as important to me as is the ongoing activity of inquiry because inquiry itself is what I deem to be sufficiently meaningful (unless I am in too much physical pain or am too incapacitated to engage in such inquiry).
