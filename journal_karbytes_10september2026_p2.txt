To Whom It May Concern:

Throughout multiple files within KARBYTES (including the relatively recent journal web page at the following Uniform Resource Locator), I have used the term "cosmic intelligence" and claimed that I was being aided by it in a manner which some people would likely describe as "divine intervention" or assistance from "a higher power". I no longer endorse using such language and prefer instead, for now on, to speak in strictly atheistic terms.

I would even go so far as to refrain from using the words "spiritual" and "spirituality" and instead use the words "philosophical" and "philosophy". I define "philosophy" as being mental activity pertaining to inquiring about what is true and what is not true (especially about the fundamental nature of reality and ontology) and which experiences are worth having and which experiences are not worth having (in pertinence to ethics and determining the order of personal priorities).

I believe that nature as a whole is most likely not sentient and hence does not impose any universal notion of ethics. I also think ethics is fundamentally context-dependent, subjective, and biased given that information processing agents each inevitably and singularly occupy some allocation of space, time, mass, and energy in a manner which prevents other information processing agents from occupying that same allotment. What I mean to imply by the previous sentence is that any organism's existence inevitably includes that organism competing with potential and presently materialized competitor organisms for some relatively scarce allocation of process-sustaining resources (and what an organism fundamentally is is an information organizing process which consumes, distributes, and restructures patterns of matter and energy). Each of such processes is a casual chain which embeds the respective information processing agent progressively deeper into the cosmos. Hence, the longer an information processing agent survives or propagates its essential information patterns, the more of reality it consumes. Even if reality is boundlessly large in dimensions such as time, space, matter, and energy, relatively atomic locations within that ultimately boundless continuum seems to have only a finite capacity to house information processing agents within them. Hence, survival is fundamentally competitive.

The purpose of this note is not to instruct readers how to behave, but rather, to inform them of my worldview and how I have decided to tighen up the vocabularly of KARBYTES to not sound as mystical or ideological as it might have in the past. I find that fully owning up to my unabashedly atheistic worldview is energizing and relieving rather than draining and conflict-ridden because I now no longer hold space in my worldview for the possibility of a favoritist divine arbitor especially given how much suffering seems to happen throughout nature. I find it more comforting to believe that nature is indifferent to an organism's suffering than believing that nature is enforcing or taking pleasure in an organism's suffering.

This note might inspire readers to not expect anything but oneself to reliably care about oneself. After all, only oneself is unconditionally present throughout its own life to endure its pain and pleasure. No one but that self could understand nor appreciate its own experiences to the same degree of intensity. Hence, one should probably stop enlisting others to take the place of its own self for the role of being someone who cares about what that self goes through or does. I think it is better to promote that each self be maximally self-reliant and to make self-befriendment (i.e. intrapersonal intimacy and advocacy) always more fundamental and higher-priority than any of its interpersonal relationships.

Sincerely,
karbytes
